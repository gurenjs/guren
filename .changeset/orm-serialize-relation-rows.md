`Model.serialize()` and `serializeMany()` apply each eager-loaded relation's own model rules to its rows, at every depth, so a related model's `hidden` columns no longer reach the output: `Post.serializeMany(await Post.with('author'))` leaves out the author's `passwordHash` when `User` hides it. A morphTo row is filtered by the model its load resolved, or, for a row no load produced, the one `Model.morphMap` names for its type. A related row follows the rule the serialized record does: when its model sets `visible`, the row keeps only the keys `visible` names, relations included, in their loaded order, each kept relation filtered by its own model; otherwise its `hidden` keys go. A related model's `appends` are not computed for relation rows. A relation declared with a synchronous lazy reference (`() => Token`) is filtered even when no load in the process has resolved it, since the serializer calls the reference; one declared with an asynchronous reference is filtered once a load or an earlier serialization has settled it, and its rows pass through as they are before that. The serialized model's own `hidden`, `visible` and `appends` behave as before, and `serializeRecord()` is unchanged.
